## 7. Advice (not an audit result)

**(a) `diff_3` Run H, rule (h) as written in GRAFT step 23 and step 24's reading note.**
1. The early arm cannot show what `main` showed. On `main` the early writes had no direct effect and acted only through the body (PD
   early −0.006, join early +0.150). At H, `dF_U` at layers 0 to 11 gave +0.031 (`diff_2`). Rule (h) evaluates "the body takes part" only
   "unless 'does not'", and "does not" prints for a mean below 0.052. So an early arm with a small total gain never gets its body
   sentence, even if (arm − its direct-path control) lies above 0. Print that difference with its interval for every arm, whatever its
   gain over H alone.
2. "Follows the item" exists only for the residual rotations. `dF_proc` at layers 0 to 11 has no other-item control in Run H, so a
   "carries" from it cannot be checked for item specificity.
3. Fit quality has no branch. `proc`'s held-out error is 0.62 to 0.89 and 1.085 at layer 15. H's embeddings and unembeddings are related
   to `main`'s only weakly (24% and 29% of the variance around the mean). A one-scale rotation of residual states is more constrained
   than either fit. A "does not" from a rotation whose held-out error is not below the mean row's says nothing about H's body. The
   reading note says this in words; a frozen "not readable" branch would make it a rule.
4. A positive "the body takes part" for a residual rotation can come from a harmful control, not only from a body that uses the write.
   That happens when PR_l, the same vector at the final norm, lowers H. Quote (PR_l − H alone) beside it.

**(b) The planned arm `PT`** (host log-probability of a candidate token plus F's minus S's teacher-forced byte log-probability of that
token's bytes; candidates from the host's top 32 and the donors' greedy continuations).

Is it the published method? No. It is a token-level reranking that approximates it. 2506.14123 turns each token model into an exact
byte-level model (the valid covering tree of its method section, which sums over every tokenization of a byte prefix). §4.4 then
combines next-byte logit vectors one byte at a time, the expert added and the anti-expert subtracted. In our setting F and S are already
byte models, so only the host needs the conversion. `PT` differs in three ways:
- the host term is one token's probability, not the host's probability of those bytes, which the published form sums over other
  tokenizations (the prompt-boundary problem the paper fixes);
- multi-byte candidates are scored by unnormalized sums, so candidates of different byte lengths are not on one scale;
- the candidate set is cut.

What would make it unfair to proxy tuning:
- the expert's preferred continuation missing from the candidates;
- a length effect that penalizes the expert's continuation;
- F's byte log-probabilities computed under boundary bits other than F's own (they must come from `_decide` and `extend`, as in F's
  decode);
- reading it as "proxy tuning" when it falls short.

The known answers it needs:
1. S as both expert and anti-expert gives the host alone's tokens exactly.
2. F's teacher-forced log-probability of its own greedy continuation equals the sum of F's per-step log-probabilities in its own decode.
3. F's greedy continuation is among the candidates at every step (asserted).
4. With the host and anti-expert terms removed, `PT` reproduces F alone's stored texts. This is the positive control for the candidate
   set and the byte scoring.
5. On `main`, an expected value set before unblinding. If `main` is close to S at the output, `PT` should land near F (0.631). A result
   far below that is first suspected as the implementation.

Name it "proxy tuning, token-candidate approximation" unless it is checked against an exact byte-level implementation on a subset.
