## 3. What crosses from F to H (claim 3)

- **The pairs alone.** `WeightsSwap.put` replaces host head (hl, hh)'s slice of o_proj's input at the deciding rows by A_F · v. Here v is the host's own values. A_F (`moved_weights`) is F's attention weights at F's matched row, moved onto the host's token positions by route_1's byte-span key map; keys that land after the host row, or have no partner, are dropped, and nothing is renormalised.
  - Nothing else of F's enters `run` for a weights-only spec. `vectors` also computes F's head outputs and mapped differences, but only `out`, `direct`, `rot` and `heads` parts use them.
  - What F contributes is a distribution over host positions: where to read, not what to write. F's prediction reaches H through that, as a pointer to the source word.
- **What F reads.** In a free-running decode, F and S read the item's prompt plus the host's own generated bytes. `Join.decode` builds them with `start` (`_decide` at answer offset 0, the prompt only) and `extend`, which re-runs the donor on the whole extended text, so the held attention inputs are full length. F never reads the gold. The gold's byte length sets the decode's stop, as for every condition.
- **Nothing is trained.** Nothing is trained in the pairs-alone wiring: `requires_grad_(False)` on every model, and no parameter is written. The pairs are a discrete choice made by greedy search on `seq`, the teacher-forced gold log-probability on the 80 fit items, among 335 single pairs a first-departure screen had kept.
- **What `out` U adds.** `out` U adds, at every layer and deciding row, F's attention-output difference from S, n_S(F_out) − n_S(S_out), through U. U is a closed-form map from `main`'s residual coordinates to H's, fitted from the unembedding matrices with ρ from the question prompts; no edit-task item is used. So the 98 carries F's own output-level difference vectors through a fitted map.

## 4. The controls (claim 4)

- **S control.** It is the same 15 pairs, the same deciding rows (the rows both F's and S's parses match, the same for either donor), the same hook and the same scale; the spec differs only in `"donor": "S"` (checked). It reads 10 and 8, level with H alone. It changes 108 of H alone's 160 texts and 56 of its 80.
- **Other-item control.**
  - Every other item is a different item of the same task, drawn inside the same list: 160 of 160 and 80 of 80, with 101 and 53 distinct others.
  - The donors read the other item's prompt (the same few-shot lines, another question line) plus the host's own answer so far. Rows of the few-shot lines map to the same donor rows, and rows of the last line map from the end.
  - So the 15 heads read the host's own text at positions taken from another item.
- **The 0 is a real effect, not a broken decode.** The texts are words of the item's own sentence in the wrong places, or loops, at 10.2 steps on average against H alone's 9.2. No answer equals the other item's gold, and 9 of 160 and 4 of 80 answers are empty. The 15 heads replaced at the deciding rows lose even H's own 12 and 6.
