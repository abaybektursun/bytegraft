A byte model running beside a token model: what of it can be moved across

Two models run on the same text. Bolmo-1B reads bytes; the receiving model reads tokens. At chosen layers and heads, at positions where both models cover the same bytes, part of the receiving model's attention is replaced by Bolmo's. The receiving model computes everything else and chooses the next token. No training; no map between the two models, except where a row names a fitted map (the output part, the residual map).

What runs at inference, in every result on this page. The whole of Bolmo-1B runs on the same text beside the receiving model, at every decoding step. Bolmo-1B-Stage1, the same model before its editing skill, also reads the text at every step: positions are paired only where both Bolmo models' patches match, and the output part and the residual map use its activations too. Bolmo works out where to look; the receiving model writes the token from its own computation. With Bolmo switched off, the receiving model is unchanged. Bolmo alone gets of 160 test items right, more than any joined system on this page. Through attention weights, what crosses into the separately trained models is a position: on Pythia's selection list, a hand-made pointer that uses the answer, with no Bolmo weights, does better than Bolmo's weights (section 13). By the study's definition of 2026-11-12 this is not yet a graft. A graft is a small part of Bolmo attached so that neither part alone does the task, or the receiving model doing the task with Bolmo switched off (the study's MISSION.md, in its working repository, not public).

2The same text enters each model in a different way

OLMo splits the text into tokens: one position per token. Bolmo reads one byte at a time, runs a small encoder over the bytes, and groups them into patches: one position per patch. A position in OLMo is paired with a position in Bolmo when both cover exactly the same bytes.

Top: OLMo position, token, token id. The token id selects one row of OLMo's embedding matrix ().
Middle: the bytes. Orange number: Bolmo's input id for that byte (byte value + 4; ids 0 to 3 are special tokens).
Bottom: Bolmo position (patch). The underlined byte is the patch's last byte: the encoder's output at that byte, after an RMSNorm and a 2048 × 2048 linear projection, is the patch's input to Bolmo's first transformer layer.

3One layer, one position: the replacement

Both models have the same layer design: 16 transformer layers, 16 attention heads of 128 dimensions, a 2048-dimensional residual stream, and an RMSNorm on each sublayer's output before it is added to the residual stream. Bolmo runs its own layer on its own residual stream. In OLMo, at a paired position, the attention output (after o_proj, before the RMSNorm) is replaced by Bolmo's.

Measured at the first generation step, at position (the last prompt position), in the run with the copy at all 16 layers. The RMSNorm applied to the copied vector is OLMo's own (its gain weights). Values in layer L already include the copies made in layers below L.

6Which part of OLMo uses the copied vectors

A control run computes, for each layer, the change the copy makes at that layer when every other layer is left alone (both against OLMo's unchanged run), and adds their sum once, at the input of OLMo's final RMSNorm. No OLMo layer reads them there. The difference between the two runs is what OLMo's own later layers (their attention and MLPs) do with the copied vectors.

7All measured results

Accuracy on the four word-edit tasks (del_word, ins_word, sub_word, swap_word), 160 items, 40 per task, strict rule. Whiskers: 95% bootstrap interval (items resampled within task, draws).

Bolmo-1B-Stage1's transformer layers are identical to OLMo's (176 tensors equal by hash); only its byte encoder and decoder were trained. Bolmo-1B continued training all of its weights. The OLMo stage-1 checkpoints are separately trained networks by their weights: for stage1-step1907359, the cosine between each of 112 weight matrices and the final model's matching matrix is within ±0.002 of zero. Copying whole attention outputs into them gives 0.000; giving 15 heads of stage1-step1907359 Bolmo's attention weights alone does not (section 12).

8Which heads carry it? The task: copy 16 of the 256

Each layer's attention output is the sum of 16 head writes: head h writes its 128 output numbers through its own 128 columns of o_proj. Copying all 16 heads at all 16 layers is the copy of sections 3 to 7. Copying one head replaces OLMo's write for that head by Bolmo's write for the same head, at the last prompt position and every generated position. The task: choose at most 16 of the 256 heads.

9Sixty scored sets per method

Each method could send 60 sets of at most 16 heads to one scorer. A dot is one set, scored once. x: the set's number, 1 to 60, in the order the method sent it. y: its score, the mean gain in the gold token's log-odds over 80 practice items (the study's fit items) when OLMo uses Bolmo's write for those heads (0: no change; 1.96: the gold token's odds multiplied by about 7; all 256 heads: ). A line is the best score so far, so it can only rise or stay flat.

The rise has two sources. More tries: the best of 60 random sets is higher than the best of 10, with no learning; random search's dots stay at one level. Learning: the agents, the surrogate and the scripts sent 20 sets, got the 20 scores back, and chose the next 20 from them; their dots move up from round to round.

Each method's final set is its own best of 60. Rounds of 20 apply to the agents, the surrogate and the scripts; random search sent its 60 sets at once. The agents also sent sets smaller than 16 as probes (Agent A 24, Agent B 19): that is why some of their early dots are low. Hover a dot for its heads.

11Where Bolmo's heads look, or what they read

One head's output at position q is WO[:, h] · Σk Ah[q, k] · vh[k]. Ah[q, k] is the attention weight from q to an earlier position k; the weights of one row sum to 1. vh[k] is the value at k, 128 numbers. WO[:, h] is the head's 128 columns of o_proj. Copying a head's output, as in sections 3 to 10, takes A, v and WO all from Bolmo. The run route_1 takes the weights from one model and the values with o_proj from the other: all 256 heads, at the last prompt position and every generated position, on OLMo-2-0425-1B.

12Into models that share no weights with Bolmo

Attention weights are indexed by position, not by the 2048 coordinates, and positions are matched by bytes. So a head of any model can be given the weights of one of Bolmo's heads, with no map: it reads its own values with Bolmo's weights, through its own o_proj. One such assignment is a pair, receiving head ← Bolmo head, used at the last prompt position and every generated position. The study tried four receiving models. H: the OLMo-2 1B checkpoint stage1-step1907359, whose weight matrices each have a cosine within ±0.002 of zero with OLMo-2-0425-1B's matching matrix, so it is a separately trained network. Pythia 1.4B: GPT-NeoX, 24 layers of 16 heads, its own tokenizer and training data. DeepSeek-Coder 1.3B: Llama type, 24 layers of 16 heads, trained on code. SmolLM2-1.7B: Llama type, 24 layers of 32 heads, a third model family.

13Through attention weights a position crosses; what else does

On these four word-edit tasks the answer is a copy of the prompt's words with one change, so where the next word sits is most of the answer. The run step 41 asks whether Bolmo's weights carry more than that. The same Pythia heads were given a hand-made pointer instead: at each answer position, all weight on the position of the word the gold answer copies next. It uses the answer and no Bolmo weights. 14The pointer in the receiving model's own weights, with Bolmo off

Section 13 found that what crosses is a position. This section asks two things with no Bolmo model running: does giving the receiving model's heads the right position fix the word-edit tasks, and can that position be put into the model's own query and key weights. Unlike every earlier section, this one trains weights of the receiving model on gold answers. Its items and scoring also differ: 600 training and 200 test items per task (800 test items on the four word-edit tasks, sentences disjoint from training), and an answer counts as right when the text before the first closing quote equals the gold after whitespace is normalized. These counts cannot be compared with the strict counts of sections 1 to 13. One seed and one split per run; no independent reader has recounted them.

What this section does not show. No LoRA or full fine-tuning baseline was run, and no learning rate was tuned per arm. No wikitext perplexity or named benchmark was measured. No closed-form write of the pointer into the weights was tried. Nothing here is a practical win over ordinary fine-tuning. Files: study/NOTES_ptr.md and results/ptr/ in this repository.

15What other research did

Each published method combines a byte model and a token model, or two models, at a different place. Pick one.

This study's row is the only one found that copies internal activations from a byte model into a token model at run time without a map or training (research passes 5 to 10 of the study; a search result, not a proof). The whole attention output works between Bolmo and the token model it was built from; copied into separately trained OLMo checkpoints it gives 0.000 (section 7). Attention weights alone do cross into separately trained models (section 12). The byte-level form of proxy tuning (ByteSampler §4.4), the published method closest to these results, has not been run beside them.

16Where the study stands

The study's scoreboard (study/SCOREBOARD.md) has two levels. Level 1: a computation of one model used by another it was never trained with, with no training, to do what the receiving model does not do alone. Each row has three answers: reuse shown (the receiving model gains and both controls fail); the joined system above its best part; the donor's part isolated (only a small part of the donor runs). Level 2: a joined system above its parts and above the ordinary method on a named benchmark.
